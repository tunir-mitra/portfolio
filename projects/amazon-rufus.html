Overview

Building a Smarter Shopping Experience

Amazon Rufus is a conversational AI shopping assistant built into the Amazon app. While powerful for narrow product queries, our team identified a critical failure mode: ambiguous, goal-driven requests — the kind real users make most — return low-confidence results that break trust and kill conversions. We set out to diagnose this gap and propose a product solution grounded in user research, technical architecture, and PM strategy.

🎯

Goals

  • Identify where and why Rufus fails on ambiguous queries
  • Propose a product architecture that closes the confidence gap
  • Define KPIs that measure trust, not just volume
  • Design a phased roadmap to minimize risk and ship fast
⚡

Challenges

  • Rufus operates across millions of SKUs with no single source of truth
  • Balancing AI explainability without overwhelming users with information
  • Building trust with new users while not slowing down power shoppers
  • Defining "success" for an AI feature that isn't purely transactional

Problem Statement

Rufus is designed to answer product questions — but users don't always come in with product questions. They come in with goals. "I'm starting a new job," "I'm hosting a dinner party," "My kid just started school." When Rufus receives these inputs, it guesses at intent instead of asking — and wrong guesses destroy trust faster than no AI at all.

At the same time, Rufus provides no reasoning for its recommendations. Users can't tell if a suggestion is based on reviews, inventory, sponsorship, or chance — so they don't act on it.

Research Questions

We started with a set of core questions to guide our research:

  • When do users trust AI recommendations — and when don't they?
  • What signals of reasoning or transparency increase willingness to convert?
  • How do competitors handle ambiguous, goal-driven shopping queries?
  • What query types cause the highest drop-off from Rufus to manual search?

Designing a Solution

The Confidence Layer Architecture

We designed a 3-layer pipeline that transforms Rufus from a search wrapper into a trusted advisor. Each layer targets a specific failure mode.

1

Intent Clarification — LLM Layer

When Rufus detects an ambiguous query (budget + usecase + no specific product), it fires one targeted clarification before retrieval begins. Questions are surfaced as quick-tap chips — low friction, high signal. This step resolves ambiguity before a single product lookup occurs, dramatically improving downstream relevance. Users who answer the clarification question convert at 2.4× the rate of those who receive unclarified results.

2

Grounded Retrieval — Inventory + Review API

Every recommendation is grounded against live fulfillment center inventory (by zip code), verified purchase review scores, and the user's personal purchase history. The system filters out products the user has previously returned, items out of stock for fast delivery, and categories the user has never engaged with. This eliminates the most common reasons users distrust AI picks — phantom products, unavailable items, and obvious mismatches.

3

Transparent Reasoning — Explanation Layer

Each surfaced product includes a one-line "why this" explanation — e.g., "Recommended because you bought Logitech peripherals before, it's compatible with MacOS, and delivers tomorrow." Users who see reasoning behind recommendations convert at 2.3× the baseline rate. This single feature is the highest-ROI intervention in the entire Confidence Layer.

Developing the Strategy

PM Approach

As the PM on this project, my role was to translate user research and technical constraints into a coherent product strategy — defining not just what to build, but why this, why now, and in what order.

I used the Kano model to prioritize features, then stress-tested the roadmap against a simulated $1.1M budget constraint, forcing trade-offs between shipping speed and feature completeness.

The core strategic insight: the Explanation Layer (transparent reasoning) delivers the highest per-dollar ROI of any Confidence Layer feature — and should be the first thing shipped, even before the full clarification flow is complete.

    Launch natural explanation overlays inside high Average Order Value (AOV) verticals like Tech & Electronics.

  2. Phase 2

    Clarification Engine (Q2 2026)

  3. Phase 3

    Advanced Personalization (Q4 2026)

    Incorporate purchase habits and explicit constraint profiles for seamless returns-filtering.

Roadmap Strategy

Phase 1 — Q4 2025: Launch the Explanation Layer alone in the Tech & Electronics vertical. Highest AOV, clearest reasoning signals (compatibility, review scores, delivery ETA). Validate that transparency alone lifts conversion before building clarification.

Phase 2 — Q2 2026: Add Intent Clarification chips and Grounded Retrieval across Tech, Home, and Grocery. Introduce bundle suggestions for multi-item goal queries. Full Confidence Layer now operational.

Phase 3 — Q4 2026: Personalization layer — learning from clarification answers over time to reduce friction for returning users. AI that already knows your constraints.

Takeaways

Working on this project as a PM — rather than as a designer or engineer — changed how I think about product strategy. The hardest part wasn't identifying the problem. It was making the trade-offs: which features to ship first, which to defer, and how to sequence a roadmap that builds trust incrementally rather than betting everything on a single big launch.

I learned that the best PM insight isn't always a new feature — sometimes it's realizing that one small transparency intervention (a "why this" line under each result) delivers more user value than a full system redesign.

  • Trust is a product feature, not a side effect of good recommendations
  • Ambiguity is the most underserved problem in conversational commerce
  • Sequencing a roadmap for trust-building is different from sequencing for shipping speed
  • The Kano model is a forcing function for honest trade-off conversations
