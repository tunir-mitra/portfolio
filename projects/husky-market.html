Overview

Closing the Trust Gap in Campus Resale

HuskyMarket is a specialized resale ecosystem built exclusively for University of Washington students. As we move into the 2026 academic cycle, we continue to solve the "trust gap" in campus commerce by mandating NetID verification and using escrow-backed payments to prevent fraud, ghosting, and quality disputes within the Husky community.

60,000+Students in the UW ecosystem
3Ways to trade: auctions, subleases, gear rentals

Understanding Campus Resale

The Current Landscape: UW Surplus

While the University provides a centralized surplus store, it caters primarily to institutional disposal rather than the dynamic, 24/7 needs of the student body. The UW Surplus Store is the current standard for university-owned item disposal, but it presents several barriers for students in 2026:

  • Limited Accessibility: Often only open for a few hours a week for public sales.
  • Logistics: No integrated peer delivery or inspection system for student-to-student sales.
  • Scope: Primarily handles university property, leaving a $2.5M gap in student-owned furniture and electronics.

The Campus Resale Calendar

Supply and demand don't show up at the same time. Seniors sell in June; the students who need their furniture arrive in September. That three-month gap is why remote buyers and hard deadlines sit at the center of the product.

  1. JuneMove-out & graduation

    Seniors like Caitlyn must clear apartments fast. Peak supply, strict deadlines.

  2. Jun – AugSummer subleases

    Interns like Dylan need a safe way to hold deposits while they're away.

  3. SeptemberMove-in

    Incoming students like Abby buy furniture they couldn't see in person. Peak demand.

  4. Dec · MarQuarter breaks

    Short-term gear rentals and room swaps between quarters.

Design

The Trust Stack

Every feature answers one of the three frictions. Stacked together, they make a stranger-to-stranger transaction feel like buying from a store.

1

Identity

Sign-in is UW NetID only, with a minimum Husky Card balance to complete verification. There are no anonymous profiles.

Solves: fraud
2

Commitment

Bidding requires a wallet balance of the bid plus $10. A bid is a promise backed by money, which makes ghosting expensive.

Solves: no-shows
3

Condition

Verified students can inspect items in person and vouch for them, alongside two-way reviews and seller history.

Solves: opaque quality
4

Payment

Winning bids and sublease deposits go into escrow. The seller sees the money is there but can't touch it yet.

Solves: payment insecurity
5

Handoff

At pickup the buyer shows a one-time code. The seller enters it, and only then is the money released. No code, no payout.

Solves: disputes

Go-to-Market

HuskyMarket is scaling to meet the needs of the 60,000+ student ecosystem at UDub. A two-sided marketplace lives or dies on its first transactions, so launch follows the campus calendar:

  1. Seed supply at move-out. Recruit graduating seniors to list before they leave in June, when their need to sell is urgent.
  2. Convert demand at move-in. Incoming students browse inspected listings over the summer and pick up in September.
  3. Grow through dorms and student orgs. Word of mouth inside a verified community is the cheapest channel we have.

Risks & Mitigations

Cold start

A marketplace needs sellers and buyers at once. Launch at move-out, when supply is guaranteed.

Holding money

Escrow has compliance rules. Run it through a licensed payments provider rather than holding funds directly.

Meetup safety

Suggest busy, public on-campus pickup zones by default.

University branding

"Husky" and UW marks need university approval before a public launch.
